US television programs carry an age-based label such as TV-PG, TV-14 or TV-MA, and often a few letters after it. The label is a guide to who the program is suitable for. The letters say why. Together they take about two seconds to read, and they are the quickest way to tell whether an episode suits the people watching with you.

## The ratings

The system is called the TV Parental Guidelines. It has been in use since 1997, and the ratings are assigned by the networks and producers who make the programs rather than by the government. Here is what each one means.

| Rating | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| TV-Y | Suitable for all children, including very young ones |
| TV-Y7 | Directed to children age 7 and older |
| TV-Y7-FV | Directed to older children, with more intense fantasy violence than TV-Y7 |
| TV-G | Suitable for all ages, though not designed specifically for children |
| TV-PG | Parental guidance suggested: some material may not suit young children |
| TV-14 | Parents strongly cautioned: may not suit children under 14 |
| TV-MA | Mature audiences only: intended for adults |

The first three ratings are for children's programs. The last four are for programs aimed at a general or adult audience.

## What the letters mean

Letters added after a rating name the reason for it:

- **D**: suggestive dialogue
- **L**: coarse or crude language
- **S**: sexual situations
- **V**: violence
- **FV**: fantasy violence (used with TV-Y7)

So "TV-14-LV" tells you that the show is intended for viewers over 14 and that the language and violence are the reasons. "TV-PG-D" is mostly fine for a family, with some suggestive talk. The letters are usually more useful than the age number, because two TV-14 shows can be very different.

## Who decides, and what happens next

The rating is applied by the program's makers or the network that airs it. A board, the TV Parental Guidelines Monitoring Board, oversees the system and reviews complaints about ratings. The system is voluntary, which is why you will sometimes disagree with a rating.

Televisions sold in the United States have included a V-chip since around the year 2000. It reads the rating sent with a program, and a parent can set the television to block anything above a chosen level. This is what the rating is for in practice: not to tell you what to think, but to let the television act on a choice you made.

## What is not rated

Some content has no TV Parental Guidelines rating. News and sports programs are normally left out, and feature films are rated by a different body, the Motion Picture Association, with ratings such as PG-13 and R. A movie shown on television may carry the TV rating of the broadcast or none at all. Live events can also go out without a rating.

## Streaming has its own habits

Many streaming services show the same labels, from TV-Y to TV-MA, on their series. Others add their own maturity ratings, age settings or content tags, and some let you create profiles for children that hide anything above a chosen level. The labels can differ between services even for the same show. If you are choosing for children, check the service's profile settings and read the content notes on the title page rather than relying on one label.

Independent organizations also publish age guidance with detailed notes about what a program contains. These can help when a rating is vague, but remember that they are opinions of the reviewers, not an official rating.

## Ratings on Cold Open are not parental ratings

On this site, a number such as 8.1 beside an episode is a viewer score from TVmaze. It is not related to TV-14 or TV-MA and says nothing about whether the program is suitable for children. ## A quick checklist for family viewing

1. Read the rating and the letters, not just the age number.
2. Look for D, S and V if you are watching with children.
3. Remember that the label is set by the makers, so a trailer is worth a look.
4. On streaming, check the service's own labels and your profile settings.
5. If it is a live event, news or sports, expect no rating at all and judge by the program.
