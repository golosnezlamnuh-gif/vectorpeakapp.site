Choosing what to watch tonight should take five minutes, not forty. The trick is to decide in a fixed order: first how much time you have, then what you already follow, then what is starting fresh, and only then everything else. This routine works on any schedule, and it works especially well on a long list of new episodes like the one on our [front page](/).

## Step 1: Start with the clock, not the titles

Before you read a single title, decide how long you can sit down. The length of an episode is the one fact that rules options out quickly.

| Time you have | What usually fits |
| --- | --- |
| About 30 minutes | Half-hour comedies, animation, short documentaries |
| About an hour | One-hour dramas, most reality and competition shows |
| An hour and a half or more | Specials, long finales, TV films |

These are rules of thumb, not guarantees, and the running time shown with each episode is the number to trust. Skipping this step is the most common reason people give up halfway through an episode on a night when they only had thirty minutes.

## Step 2: Check what you already follow

If you have followed shows on [My shows](/watchlist/), look there first. Your own shows, sorted by what airs next, are a shorter and better list than the whole schedule. An episode of something you already like beats an unknown episode almost every time, and it needs no research.

If you do not follow anything yet, skip to the next step. You can build the list as you go.

## Step 3: Look for a fresh start

When nothing on your list is on, look for the labels. A **series premiere** is the first episode of a brand-new show, which means no homework: nobody expects you to know what happened before. A **season premiere** is a good choice only for shows you already know. Our guide to [premiere labels](/guides/premiere-types-explained/) explains where each one makes a good starting point.

## Step 4: Narrow the list with the filters

On the schedule, the genre buttons narrow the day to the kind of thing you are in the mood for: drama, comedy, documentary, and so on. The box labeled "Include talk, news and sports" is off by default, because those programs would otherwise fill the page. Switch it on only if that is what you want tonight.

Narrowing by genre first, and reading titles second, keeps the list short enough to scan in under a minute.

## Step 5: Read the brief, but know what it is

The episodes at the top of the day have a short brief: the channel, the time, the length, the rating if there is one, and a summary from the broadcaster or TVmaze. A brief is not a review. Episodes are ranked by a published formula that adds up the show's popularity, a bonus for premieres and a small credit for good ratings. You can read the full method on the [how it works](/how-it-works/) page. Use the brief to answer two questions: is this the kind of thing I am in the mood for, and does it fit my time?

A note on ratings: the number shown comes from TVmaze viewers. A score based on a handful of votes says little, and a new show may have no score at all. Treat it as a tie-breaker, not as a verdict.

## Step 6: Settle clashes in one move

Sometimes two things you want start at the same time. Decide which one is a live event and which can wait. Finales, sports and award shows lose most of their value if you watch them late, because the result reaches you first. A regular episode of a drama keeps. Watch the live one now and save the other for later.

Streaming releases rarely clash with anything, because they are available whenever you are ready. Those with no fixed release time sit under "All day" on the schedule.

## Step 7: Decide, then set a reminder

Pick one episode and commit. If something else looks good, press "Add to calendar" on it, or follow the show, so that it is waiting for you tomorrow instead of nagging you tonight.

## Mistakes that waste the evening

- **Scrolling without a goal.** Decide your time and mood before you look at titles.
- **Starting mid-season.** A show in its sixth season is a poor first date. Choose a premiere or a show built from standalone episodes.
- **Trusting a rating alone.** A high score from very few voters is not a promise.
- **Choosing a live event too late.** If the result is already online, the main reason to watch has gone.

Five minutes of this routine is usually enough. If you only remember one thing, remember the order: time first, your own shows second, fresh starts third.
