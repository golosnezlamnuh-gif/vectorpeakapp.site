Television has a vocabulary of its own, and many of the words show up in listings and entertainment news without explanation. This glossary covers the terms you are most likely to meet, in alphabetical order, with a short example of each in use.

## A to C

**Anthology series.** A show where each episode, or each season, tells a different, self-contained story, often with a new cast. *Black Mirror* is an anthology of standalone episodes, and *American Horror Story* tells a new story each season.

**Bottle episode.** An episode set mostly in one location with a small cast, often to save money or to focus on character and dialogue. A bottle episode usually has few effects and many conversations.

**Cliffhanger.** An ending that leaves a major question open, so that viewers return for the answer. Season finales are the usual home for cliffhangers.

**Cold open.** A scene that plays before the opening credits, designed to hook the viewer in the first minute. The long-running sketch show *Saturday Night Live* is known for its cold open, which ends with the line "Live from New York, it's Saturday night!". It is also where this site gets its name.

## E to L

**Episode order.** The number of episodes a network or service commits to for a season, as in "a ten-episode order". It tells you how long a season will be.

**Finale.** The final episode. A season finale ends a season, and a series finale ends the whole show.

**Hiatus.** A planned break in the run of a show, often around the winter holidays. The show is not canceled. It returns after the gap.

**Limited series.** A story with a planned ending, told in one season. A limited series is not expected to return with the same story, and it is sometimes called a miniseries.

**Linear TV.** Programs shown at a scheduled time on a channel, as opposed to on-demand viewing. A listing such as "Thursday at 9 p.m." describes linear TV.

## M to R

**Mid-season finale and mid-season premiere.** The last episode before a break within a season and the first one after it. Together they bracket a hiatus.

**Pilot.** The first episode produced for a show, made to introduce the idea to the network or service. Not every pilot becomes a series. When one does, it is often the same episode that airs as the series premiere.

**Preemption.** Replacing a scheduled program with something else, such as a live event or breaking news. See [why your show wasn't on](/guides/why-tv-schedules-change/) for the common causes.

**Procedural.** A show where each episode follows a self-contained case, investigation or problem, which is usually solved by the end. You can start watching a procedural almost anywhere.

**Recap.** A short summary of earlier events at the start of an episode, often introduced by "Previously on...".

**Renewal.** A network or service ordering another season of a show. The opposite is a cancellation.

## S to U

**Season and series.** In the United States, a season is a group of episodes and a series is the whole show. In Britain, "series" often means what Americans call a season. On this site, "Series premiere" always means the first episode of a show.

**Showrunner.** The person with day-to-day creative and managerial responsibility for a series, usually a writer and producer. The showrunner is often the person who has the final say on scripts.

**Simulcast.** A program broadcast at the same time on two or more channels or platforms.

**Spin-off.** A new series built around characters or a world from an existing show.

**Sweeps.** Periods, traditionally in November, February and May, when audience size is measured in more detail and used to set local advertising prices. Networks have historically saved big episodes and events for these months.

**Upfronts.** Annual presentations, usually held in spring, where networks and streaming services show advertisers the programs they plan to release. They are where networks present their coming schedules and many new series.

## Streaming words

**Binge release.** A season released all at once. See [how streaming shows are released](/guides/streaming-release-patterns/) for the other patterns.

**FAST channel.** A free, ad-supported streaming channel, designed to feel like a traditional channel with a schedule.

**Streamer.** A service that delivers programs over the internet, such as a subscription video service.

## Using the glossary

Most of these words describe where an episode sits in a bigger plan.
