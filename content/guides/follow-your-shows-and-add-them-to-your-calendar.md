You can follow any show on Cold Open with one button, see when each followed show airs next, and add episodes to your calendar. There is no account to create. The list is kept in your browser, and the calendar files work with Google Calendar, Apple Calendar, Outlook and most other calendar apps.

## Follow a show

1. Open [today's schedule](/) or the [premieres page](/premieres/), or use [search](/search/) to find a show by name.
2. Press **Follow** on an episode or on the show's page.
3. A message confirms that you are following it.

That is all. You can unfollow in the same place.

## What "My shows" tells you

The [My shows](/watchlist/) page lists everything you follow, sorted by what airs next. For each show you see the next episode and its air time converted to your own time zone. It is the shortest route to the question "what can I watch this week?", because it contains only shows you already chose.

If a followed show has nothing scheduled yet, it still appears, so you do not lose track of it between seasons.

## Add a single episode to your calendar

Any episode with a fixed air time has an **Add to calendar** button. Press it and your browser downloads a small file ending in .ics. Open that file, and your calendar app offers to add the event. The event is set at the right moment for your time zone, so you do not have to convert anything.

This is handy for one-off events: a series premiere you do not want to forget, or a live special.

## Add everything you follow at once

On the My shows page, the button **Add upcoming episodes to your calendar (.ics)** puts the next scheduled episode of every show you follow into one file. Import it and your calendar shows what is coming up for all of your shows.

How you import a file depends on the calendar:

- **Google Calendar:** open Settings, choose Import and export, pick the file and the calendar it should go into.
- **Apple Calendar:** open the downloaded file, or choose File and then Import.
- **Outlook:** choose File, then Open and Export, then Import/Export, and select the file.

The menu names change from time to time, so look for "import" if the wording differs. An imported file is a copy: if a network changes a time later, the event in your calendar does not update. Re-download the file near the date if the time matters.

## Know what stays in your browser

Your list is stored in your browser on the device you are using. It is not sent to a server and it is not tied to an account. That has two consequences you should know about:

- **It does not sync.** If you follow shows on your phone, they will not appear on your laptop. Follow them on each device you use.
- **It can disappear.** Clearing your browser's site data, or using a private window, removes the list. If you rely on a list, add the upcoming episodes to your calendar as a second copy.

## Following shows from other countries

The schedule has regions for the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia. Switch regions above the schedule to see what is on there, and follow shows from any of them. Each region's days follow its own broadcast calendar, while the times are always shown in your time zone.

## A simple weekly routine

A routine takes two minutes and saves most of the scrolling.

1. Once a week, open [My shows](/watchlist/) and look at what is coming.
2. Download the calendar file and import it. It holds the next episode of each show, so a weekly download keeps your calendar current.
3. On the evening itself, look at the [schedule](/) only if you have a free night and nothing on your list.

If you are unsure what to watch on a free night, [our five-minute routine](/guides/how-to-pick-what-to-watch-tonight/) shows how to choose quickly. And if a time looks wrong, [our guide to reading a schedule](/guides/how-to-read-a-tv-schedule/) explains time zones step by step.
